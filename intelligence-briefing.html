Brent Crude

96.28 USD/bbl · 4 Sep
2025 2026 War escalation, 28 Feb 2026

Monthly close · Source: investing.com/commodities/brent-oil-historical-data

LOOKING AHEAD

The EIA has revised its Brent forecast up and down several times through 2026 as the war has escalated and eased — from an average of $56/bbl for the year before the war, to as high as $95/bbl mid-year when Middle East shut-ins peaked, before cutting back toward $82/bbl as disruptions eased, then lifting it again in a later report citing fresh supply risk. The throughline across every revision: 2027 is still expected to land lower than 2026, in the $60s-70s, as global oversupply reasserts itself once the conflict's shipping and production disruptions clear.

Source: EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook, Jan–Aug 2026 editions (via Hydrocarbon Engineering, Rigzone, Ship & Bunker)

Sugar

523.10 USD/MT · 6 Sep
2025 2026 War escalation, 28 Feb 2026

Jan–Aug 2025: month-end close · Sep 2025–Aug 2026: monthly average of weekly closes · Source: investing.com/commodities/london-sugar

LOOKING AHEAD

The supply glut behind 2025's decline is shrinking faster than expected. Trader Czarnikow cut its 2026/27 global surplus estimate from 3.4 million tonnes in February to just 1.1 million tonnes by late April, citing Brazil's accelerating shift of cane toward ethanol and a severe rainfall deficit in Thailand's own cane belt threatening the next ratoon crop. A smaller cushion means less room to absorb any further war-driven ethanol demand — but the surplus hasn't flipped to a deficit, so a ceasefire and falling oil could still pull prices back down toward 2025 levels.

Source: Czarnikow via Barchart, 27 Apr 2026; CropGPT Sugar Monthly Report, Apr 2026

LOOKING AHEAD

Multiple market analysts frame 2026 as structurally tight independent of the war: China's smelter output is approaching its 45-million-tonne capacity ceiling, and the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism takes full effect in 2026, with scrap export tariffs expected to squeeze secondary supply from spring onward. Demand from solar and EV manufacturing is also cited as a steady floor. SMM's research summary describes the likely path as a "breakthrough and return" year — meaning elevated volatility is more probable than a clean retreat to 2025 levels, even if the war itself de-escalates.

Source: SMM / AlCircle, "LME aluminium prices hit a three-year high in 2025 — overseas views on 2026 outlook," 28 Nov 2025

Resin — PET

2025 2026 War escalation, 28 Feb 2026

Week-4 mean of each month, China ($/MT) column · Source: CEPG Asia PET weekly report, week 31 2026 edition · Aug 2026 onward pending a newer report

LOOKING AHEAD

PET's feedstock chain (paraxylene → PTA → PET) keeps it tied to crude in the near term, so further oil moves from the war remain the dominant swing factor through the rest of 2026. Independent of the war, industry data points to a structural easing ahead: Asia is set to add roughly 47.6 million tonnes of new PTA capacity by 2027 — 89% of all global additions — which should widen feedstock supply and cap how far prices can run once new plants ramp up. IMARC's Q2 2026 tracking already shows China PET easing to around $953/MT as post-holiday supply normalized, consistent with the pullback visible in this briefing's own data from April's peak.

Source: GlobalData, "Asia to lead global PTA capacity additions by 2027," 2024; IMARC Group PET Resin Price Trend, Q2 2026, 21 Jul 2026

USD/THB

2025 2026 War escalation, 28 Feb 2026

Monthly close · Source: investing.com/currencies/usd-thb-historical-data

LOOKING AHEAD

Commerzbank's FX desk notes the Bank of Thailand explicitly attributes recent baht volatility to Middle East developments and shifting Fed rate expectations, rather than domestic factors. The BoT has held its policy rate at 1.00% for three straight meetings and is expected to stay on hold into the first half of 2027. With no rate move expected from either side, Commerzbank calls for USD/THB to consolidate in a 32.50–33.00 range near-term — meaning further big swings would most likely come from another external shock, not a change in Thai monetary policy.

Source: Commerzbank via FXStreet, 27 Aug 2026
